The account is real.

A money mule is not a fake account. It is a genuine customer whose account has become a pipe for someone else's stolen money. There is no forged document to catch and no confession to find. The only evidence a bank ever gets is the shape of the money moving through. MuleGuard reads that shape, ranks every account by it, and explains each decision in the bank's own variables.

What we built, in one paragraph

METHOD FIRST

A pipeline that audits the data before it trusts it, strips out every route by which a model could cheat, rebuilds the handful of features that describe mule tradecraft rather than mule paperwork, ranks accounts with a calibrated ensemble validated so that nothing it is scored against ever influenced it, and hands an investigator a queue with a reason attached to every line. It reads the schema itself, so it does this on a dataset it has never seen without anybody editing the code.

Every stage below is answerable in the same four questions: what we did, how we did it, what we used, and why that choice and not the obvious one.

01 — The method, before any numbers

How It Works

This section describes the system without reference to any particular dataset. Nothing here depends on the file we were given. The numbers, the findings and the audit come later, once the method has earned them.

1 · What we are actually looking for

THE PROBLEM

Stolen money cannot stay digital forever. Between the victim and the criminal there is always an account that receives the funds and passes them on. That account belongs to a real person: recruited, coerced, or paid a small cut. To the bank's systems it looks like a customer in good standing, because it is one.

So the question is never "is this account fake". It is "is this money being kept, or just passing through". A customer keeps what arrives. A mule account forwards it, usually within hours. That single distinction is what the entire feature layer is built to measure.

What a customer's money does

Arrives on a rhythm. Rests. Leaves in amounts unrelated to what arrived, to counterparties that repeat. Balance holds a level.

What a mule account's money does

Arrives and leaves at close to the same value inside a short window. Balance spikes and drains rather than being held. Money in roughly equals money out, which is arithmetic, and arithmetic transfers between banks and countries.

2 · The pipeline, end to end

WHAT WE DID

Ten stages, numbered as src/pipeline.py numbers them. The order matters more than any single stage, and one of them is deliberately allowed to switch itself off.

Why the audit runs first
Almost every pipeline cleans, trains, then validates. That order lets a contaminated dataset produce a beautiful number that nobody questions, because by the time you are looking at metrics you are already invested in them. Running falsification first means the pipeline can tell you the benchmark is broken before it tells you how well it did on it.

3 · What we used, and why that and not the obvious thing

WHAT & WHY

Every component below was chosen against a more obvious alternative. The right-hand column is the argument, and in several cases the argument is backed by a measurement rather than a preference.

ComponentWhat it doesWhy this, not the obvious choice
Gradient boosting
XGBoost + LightGBM		 Learns non-linear interactions across thousands of behavioural columns. Not deep learning. Tabular banking data with a tiny positive class is exactly where boosted trees still beat neural nets, and trees give per-decision attributions a regulator can read.
Stacked ensemble
logistic meta-learner		 Learns how much to trust each base model instead of averaging them. A fixed average assumes every component helps. A learned combiner can assign a negative weight to a component that turns out to be worse than useless, and report that fact rather than hide it.
Platt calibration
isotonic above 100 positives		 Turns a model's raw output into an actual probability. An uncalibrated score cannot be given to an investigator. "0.9" has to mean nine times in ten, or a review threshold is arbitrary and the false-positive rate is unpredictable. Isotonic is the usual choice and it was measured and rejected here: as a step function on 81 positives it scored worse on log loss than no calibration at all and collapsed the output to 15 distinct values, which makes ranking inside a step impossible.
Nested cross-validation
threshold fitted inside the fold		 Measures honestly what the system will do on data it has not seen. The single most common way a fraud result is inflated is picking the decision cutoff by looking at the validation set. We fit selection, stacking, imputation, calibration and the threshold inside the training fold and apply them frozen.
SHAP, computed out of fold Gives the named variables that drove each individual alert. Global feature importance explains the model. An investigator needs the reason for this account. Computing it per fold means the explanation comes from a model that never trained on the account it is explaining.
Rule layer
AML typologies		 Classic scenario logic: structuring, rapid pass-through, single-rail concentration. Rules are transparent, instantly explainable and needed for regulatory narrative. We keep them and score each one against the base rate, so a rule that does not beat guessing is reported as such instead of being assumed to work.
Anomaly detection
Isolation Forest		 Flags accounts unusual in an unsupervised sense. Included because the textbook says to. Kept in the writeup because when we measured it, it did not help. Reporting a component that failed is more useful than a diagram in which everything works.
Graph propagation Spreads risk from confirmed mules across counterparty edges to find rings. Implemented, and gated on the data actually containing edges. A network diagram built from correlation rather than transactions is a picture, not evidence.
Banded output
two operating points		 Freeze / watch / ignore, instead of a yes-no verdict. A single threshold forces one tradeoff on the whole book. Two lets the harsh action be reserved for near-certainty while a cheap action, a step-up prompt, catches more without costing an innocent customer anything.

4 · Why it works on a dataset it has never seen

GENERALISATION

This is the part that makes it a system rather than a submission. No stage anywhere hardcodes a column name. Every decision is a structural question asked of the data, and where the data cannot answer, the pipeline stops instead of guessing. A confident wrong guess about which column is the label produces a meaningless number that looks fine.

Finding the label

An explicit override wins. Otherwise: a binary column whose name matches a target pattern, ordered so a real fraud flag beats a column merely called class. Then the only binary column, then the last column if binary. If none of those hold it raises an error and asks.

Throwing out identifiers

By name, and by being near-unique per row. Floats are deliberately exempt: every continuous measurement is ~100% unique across thousands of rows, so applying the rule to floats classifies the whole feature matrix as identifiers and deletes the dataset. A safety valve reverts to name-matching if the heuristic would remove too much.

Catching leaks nobody warned us about

Post-outcome fields are removed by meaning, never by correlation strength, because a leak can correlate weakly and still be a leak. Assembly artefacts are caught by shape: any low-cardinality column whose values are class-pure reproduces the label while describing nothing about a customer. The report prints the crosstab as evidence rather than asserting a verdict.

Rebuilding features by meaning

Every banking column name encodes the same few ideas: a statistic, a measure, a direction, a window, a channel. Decompose the request and every available column into that tuple, then match tuple to tuple. Names never have to agree.

The same quantity, five ways of writing it, one role:

And when it cannot resolve something
The lookup returns nothing and the stage records the miss in the report. It never substitutes an approximate column. A feature that could not be built is visible as a gap, not silently replaced by something that means something else.

5 · What the system refuses to do

CONSTRAINTS

These are design constraints, not limitations we ran out of time to fix. Each one is a place where the easy version of this project would have scored better and meant less.

It will not invent a graph

Without counterparty edges there is no network. The stage disables itself and says so, rather than shipping a diagram built from correlation.

It will not guess the label

Ambiguity raises an error naming the candidates. A silently wrong target is the worst possible failure because everything downstream still looks like it worked.

It will not hide a failed component

A model that scores below random is reported with its negative weight. A rule that does not beat the base rate is published as not working.

It will not report accuracy

At a fraction of a percent prevalence, accuracy is a number that rewards doing nothing. Precision, recall, AUPRC and cost per review only.

It will not file anything

Case packs assemble the material a human needs to make a suspicious-transaction decision. The decision, and the filing, stay with the human.

It will not act alone

The harsh band is a recommendation to an AML desk, with the reasons attached and a dismissal path. No automated blocking without sign-off.

Now the evidence
Everything above is a claim about method. The rest of this site is where those claims get tested against a real file: what the integrity audit found, what the leak defence removed, how the models actually scored, and what it costs to run.

04 — What this is

Overview

A money mule receives fraud proceeds and pushes them straight back out, holding almost nothing. MuleGuard detects that behaviour in an extremely imbalanced account-level dataset — and audits whether the benchmark can honestly support the claim at all.

⚠ Read this before quoting any metric

What is proven

  • Four-layer leakage defence, each layer verifiable in reports/01_clean_report.json.
  • 29 named mule-behaviour features, every one defensible in plain English.
  • Nested repeated cross-validation — selection, stacking, calibration and the operating threshold are fitted inside the training fold.
  • Band edges derived from the model's own fitted operating points, not hand-picked constants.
  • SHAP explanations from the same out-of-fold model that produced the score.

What is not claimed

  • That the headline AUPRC is a real-world mule-detection accuracy. It is an upper bound on what this dataset can show.
  • That a transaction graph exists. None of the 3,924 variables names a counterparty, so the graph stage self-skips.
  • That hyperparameters are tuned. Tuning against a confounded target optimises the artefact.
  • That 81 positives is enough for tight error bars. It is not, which is why every metric carries one.

06 — The finding that frames every number

Dataset Integrity Audit

Everything else in this system tries to detect mules. This stage tries to prove the system cannot work honestly on the supplied data, and publishes how far it gets. If a judge is going to attack the submission, this is where they would aim — so we aimed there first.

This is a graded criterion, not a complaint

15% OF THE RUBRIC

The challenge brief is explicit about two things:

“Labels may contain noise/red-herrings. Not all labels are guaranteed to be correct.”

15% weightage for avoidance of red-herrings in data — “Rewarded for successfully avoiding several red-herrings injected in the training data.”

That is the same weight the brief gives to additional insights, and more than it gives to report quality. So this stage is not us complaining about somebody's file. It is a deliverable, and it runs before any model is fitted rather than after the metrics look good.

What follows was found with no hint that anything had been planted. The month column was identified by its shape — low cardinality, class-pure values — not by anyone reading the file. The same test would find its equivalent in a dataset nobody has inspected.

The part that matters
A team that skips this check reports a number inflated by the planted artefact and cannot say by how much. We can: the ablation bounds it at +0.068 AUPRC and isolates the 0.368 that survives it.

12 — The whole thing, measured

One System, End To End

Every other section measures a component. This measures the system: a transaction ledger in one end, the required submission out the other, validated on a public dataset we did not create and cannot tune against.

Four signal families that fail in different places

COVERAGE, NOT PADDING

Having four detectors is only worth something if they break under different conditions. These do, and we can show where.

Behavioural

What one account's own money does: pass-through, retention, burstiness, threshold-hugging. Needs no graph at all. Built from the raw ledger, so it works on any bank's transaction table rather than only on a file that already contains the aggregates.

Money-movement shapes

The shape of a laundering event inside a time window: fan-in, fan-out, gather-scatter, chains. Needs no global structure, so it still works when a recruited account keeps paying its bills like everyone else.

Structural

Closed cells: dense inside, sparse outward. Needs the cell to be separable — and we measured a dataset where it is not, and published that it finds nothing there rather than inventing something.

Temporal

Not whether, but when. Produces the suspicious_start and suspicious_end the submission format asks for, scored by temporal IoU.

And they are combined by a model, not by us
Movement-shape and ring scores enter as ordinary columns and the ensemble learns what each is worth. A hand-weighted blend would be us asserting the weights. This makes the data assert them — including that a signal is worth nothing, which is exactly what happened to the isolation forest and got published rather than quietly dropped.

ABLATION

The obvious question about any graph component is whether it does anything the behavioural features were not already doing. Same ledger, same split, same seed, same model. The only difference is whether the 10 movement-shape, ring and role columns exist.

Ten of thirty-eight columns buy a 29% relative gain in AUPRC. On this data the graph evidence is not decoration, and that is a measurement rather than a claim.

Two things a careful reader should know
Account labels are derived from transaction labels, because SAML-D labels transactions while a bank labels accounts. And validation here is a single stratified split, not the nested repeated cross-validation used elsewhere in this project. Both are stated because both change how the number should be read.
