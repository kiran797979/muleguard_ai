01  Executive Summary

Money mules are the “cash-out” layer of almost every digital fraud — the accounts that receive stolen funds and pass them onward. Banks must catch them precisely (a wrongly frozen account is a harmed customer) and early. MuleGuard is an end-to-end, reproducible pipeline that scores every account 0–1000, auto-freezes the highest-risk tier, and explains every decision — while refusing to cheat on the data.

The problem, precisely

The hackathon dataset is a 9,082-account × 3,923-feature matrix with only 81 confirmed mules (0.89%) and fully anonymised columns (F1…F3924). At this imbalance, “accuracy” is meaningless — predicting everyone is clean already scores 99.1%. The real task is ranking 81 needles out of 9,082, precisely, without leaking.

What MuleGuard delivers

A calibrated XGBoost model reaching AUPRC 0.934 and an out-of-sample operating point of 93.4% precision at 86.1% recall (catches ~70/81 mules), a 0–1000 risk score with a 3-tier action policy, per-account SHAP explanations, and a working inference path for new accounts.

The differentiator — measured honesty. Two hidden target leaks were found and removed; the feared “sampling-month” confound was proven harmless by measurement (removing all calendar features changes AUPRC by −0.0009); and the headline operating point is estimated by repeated nested cross-validation, so the decision threshold is never chosen on the data it is then scored on. Removing that optimism raised the real recall from 0.802 → 0.861.

02  The Problem & Business Context

Business PotentialInnovation

What a money mule is — and why it is a bank's problem

A money mule is a bank account — often opened with genuine KYC by a willing, coerced, or duped holder — that exists to move someone else's criminal proceeds. Scam and phishing payouts land in the mule, then scatter across layers of onward transfers until the trail dissolves. That makes the mule the single highest-leverage interdiction point in the chain: freeze it and the stolen money stops before it fragments and leaves the country. Miss it and a bank pays three times over — the direct loss, regulatory exposure under RBI mule-account directions and PMLA, and lasting reputational harm. The modelling challenge is stark: 81 confirmed mules hidden among 9,082 accounts — a 0.89% prevalence — with 3,923 anonymised features concealing subtle leaks and error costs that are steeply asymmetric.

Why it is genuinely hard

  • Needle-scale imbalance — 81 confirmed mules among 9,082 accounts (0.89%) makes "accuracy" a trap: flagging every account as normal scores 99.1% while catching zero mules.
  • Anonymised feature soup — 3,923 opaque F#### columns forbid hand-picking "velocity" or "device" intuitions, so every predictive signal had to be discovered, not assumed.
  • Deceptive target leaks — F3912 (0.969 Pearson correlation) and F2230 (sampling month, where every normal is Oct25 and every mule Sep/Nov/Dec25) each faked near-perfect scores; left in, they would top the leaderboard and collapse in production.
  • Asymmetric error cost — a wrongly frozen account punishes a real customer, so recall gains must be weighed against the 4.9 average false positives per fold.

The business value MuleGuard creates

  • Automated first line — the 97.2%-precise HIGH band can auto-freeze with near-zero false freezes.
  • Analyst force-multiplier — a high-recall queue focuses scarce investigators on ~real cases.
  • Audit-ready — every flag carries SHAP reasons + a calibrated score for the STR file.
  • Cheap to run — ≈5 min on a laptop; no GPU, no cloud dependency.

Where MuleGuard sits in the bank's fraud-operations flow

Figure 1 — MuleGuard as the triage layer between raw account data and human/automated action. Counts are the measured band distribution on the real dataset.
Honest scope. MuleGuard scores accounts from an account-level snapshot. True mule rings also live in the transaction graph (who-paid-whom). The provided data has no such edges, so the graph stage self-skips rather than fabricating them — and is pre-wired to activate the day a transaction ledger is supplied (see §9, §11). This is a deliberate honesty choice, and a clean growth path.

Market context

India runs the world's highest-volume retail payment rails: UPI clears billions of transactions monthly, and mule accounts are the cash-out layer fraud proceeds flow through. Our two-tier policy fits that scale. The HIGH band (69/71 accounts, 97.2% precision) triggers near-zero-false-positive auto-freeze and STR filing into the FIU-IND workflow, while a high-recall analyst queue (out-of-sample recall 0.861) catches the distributed remainder without drowning reviewers. Because the signal is spread across 1,614 features — a top-5-features-only model scores just AUROC 0.826 — there is no single rule to hard-code, which is exactly why a calibrated scorer earns its place. It ships as a plug-in batch scorer alongside existing core-banking overnight jobs: no GPU, ~5-minute deterministic run (seed=42) from one CSV. Per-account feature attribution and a seeded, auditable pipeline give compliance and RBI examiners the defensible "why" behind every freeze. At 0.89% prevalence with unverified negatives, precision is a lower bound.

Categorical recovery — signal others would destroy

8 columns are genuine strings (account type, occupation, gender, segment, recency, status, open-date). Naïve numeric coercion silently turns them to NaN and throws away the richest semantic signal. MuleGuard encodes them with generic rules:

  • Low-cardinality (≤30) → one-hot dummies (40 columns recovered)
  • High-cardinality dates → numeric “vintage” (days since open)
  • Perfect-separation categoricals → dropped as leaks (see below)

The innovation: a generic categorical leak-guard

Our core innovation is a categorical leak-guard that replaces brittle blocklists with a single generic test. Rather than hard-coding drop F2230, it interrogates every categorical column: does knowing the category reduce majority-class error by 0.98 or more? If so, the column is dropped before one-hot encoding, so no surviving dummy subset can smuggle the signal back in. This caught F2230 — the sampling-month artefact where every normal account sat in Oct25 and every mule in Sep, Nov, or Dec25 — with zero prior knowledge of it. It also independently removed F3912, but through a separate Pearson correlation scan (0.969 with the target), not this test. It is an automated integrity check that generalises to unseen leaks, not a manual patch fitted to one column.

Figure 3 — The leak-guard decision path. The test is generic (error-reduction ≥ 0.98), so it caught the month leak automatically — not because anyone told it to.
Why this matters to a judge. The F2230 month leak is a perfect trap: every normal account is Oct25, every mule is Sep/Nov/Dec25. A model that “learned” the month would report ~100% here and fail completely in production, where every live account shares the current month. MuleGuard removes it automatically — and then proves the removal was sufficient (next page).

05  The Honesty Story — Proven, Not Argued

InnovationTechnical Feasibility

Dropping the month column is necessary but not sufficient — a confound can hide across many weak features. So MuleGuard measures whether any residual leak survives. This is the section a judge will probe hardest, and every claim here is a number from reports/06_leak_audit.json.

Test 1 — Remove ALL calendar features

If the 0.93 ceiling secretly rode on the sampling month, deleting every calendar/recency feature should collapse it. Measured:

AUPRC with calendar features0.9133
AUPRC without them0.9142
Change−0.0009

Verdict: ROBUST. Removing the calendar entirely changes nothing. The result does not depend on the month cohort.

Test 2 — Can 5 features reconstruct the label?

A concentrated leak would let a tiny cluster of columns hit ~0.99 AUROC. A model trained on only the top-5 features scores:

Full model AUROC
0.990
Top-5-only AUROC
0.826

Verdict: DISTRIBUTED. No small cluster memorises the label. The 0.99 is genuine multivariate signal — many moderate features combining.

Two target leaks were caught & removed — the honest audit trail

LeakWhat it wasHow it was caughtStatus
F3912A near-duplicate of the label — Pearson correlation 0.969 with the target.Correlation scan (|corr| > 0.90)REMOVED
F2230The sampling month. All normals Oct25; all mules other months — a dataset-assembly artifact, not fraud signal.Generic categorical leak-guard (error-reduction ≥ 0.98)REMOVED
The limit we state openly (not around). A forward temporal split is infeasible by construction: since all normals are Oct25 and all mules are non-October, no time-split has both classes on each side. We disclose this rather than fabricate a temporal holdout — and it is exactly why the audit above (which does not need a time axis) is the right proof.

Our design principle, in our own words

We built MuleGuard under one rule: an honest number a judge can trust beats an inflated one that collapses under questioning. In practice that meant reporting the out-of-sample nested-CV headline (AUPRC 0.9345, recall 0.861) instead of the flattering in-sample ceiling (recall 0.901), and pairing every metric with a 95% confidence interval — AUPRC lands at 0.9345 with CI 0.887-0.977. Our leak audit is measured, not asserted: dropping every calendar and recency feature shifts AUPRC by -0.0009, and a top-5-features-only model still scores AUROC 0.826 — so the signal is distributed, not one concentrated leak. We state plainly this is a Positive-Unlabeled problem — negatives are unverified — so precision 0.934 is a lower bound. And the graph stage self-skips when no month spans both classes rather than fabricate edges. Numbers that survive scrutiny.

Honest by construction — the six habits behind every number

① AUPRC, never accuracy

At 0.89% prevalence a do-nothing model scores 99.1%. We rank with AUPRC and report precision/recall — the metrics that actually reward finding mules.

② Out-of-sample operating point

The threshold and calibrator are fit on inner folds and scored on an untouched outer fold, so the headline can't quietly borrow from the data it grades (§7).

③ Positive-Unlabeled candour

The 81 mules are confirmed; the ~9,001 “normals” are merely un-investigated. So our precision is a floor, not a ceiling — we say so rather than imply certainty.

④ The graph refuses to guess

With no who-paid-whom columns present, Stage ④ writes a skip note instead of inventing an edge list. A blank is more honest than a fabrication.

⑤ Uncertainty travels with the number

Every headline ships with a 95% interval and cross-seed spread; the wide 0.25–1.00 threshold range at 81 positives is disclosed, not buried.

⑥ The code was audited too

An adversarial review of our own pipeline caught a real one-hot imputation bug in the scoring path; we fixed it and pinned it with a test (§8/§9).

07  Results — The Honest Headline

Business PotentialTechnical Feasibility

Our headline is an out-of-sample AUPRC of 0.9345 (95% CI 0.887-0.977) — measured on outer folds the model never saw during fitting or threshold selection. At the operating point it runs Precision 0.934 and Recall 0.861, catching a mean of 69.7 of the 81 confirmed mules against only 4.9 false positives per fold. The nested CV is what makes this hard to game: every decision threshold is chosen on inner folds and scored on a held-out outer fold, so the number can't borrow information from the data it grades. The counterintuitive part is that removing the earlier in-sample optimism raised recall from 0.802 to 0.861 — the max_depth=3 and scale_pos_weight-only fixes lifted the whole PR curve (0.9133 to 0.9301 across 5 seeds), not just one point. We report honestly: with only 81 positives, per-fold thresholds spread from 0.25 to 1.00.

What makes the UX work for a bank

  • One number anyone reads. 0–1000 with three colour-coded bands — no ML literacy needed.
  • Reasons, not black box. Ranked SHAP drivers per account satisfy RBI/PMLA “explainable decision” duties.

  • Two machine formats. Human-readable .txt for analysts; JSON twin for case-management systems.
  • One-command operation. python src/pipeline.py runs everything; python src/score_new.py new.csv scores fresh accounts.
